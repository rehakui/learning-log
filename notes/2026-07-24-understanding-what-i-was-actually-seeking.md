# TIL - Understanding What I Was Actually Seeking

Today I recognized several patterns that had been repeating for years.

The strongest dopamine wasn't coming from the experience itself—it came from the anticipation: searching, comparing, imagining, and building expectations in my mind. Once the experience became real, the excitement consistently faded much faster than I expected.

I also realized that physical attraction alone isn't enough for me. What I truly value is a sense of connection, trust, and knowing the other person. Without that, the experience feels emotionally interchangeable, regardless of who the other person is.

Another important observation is that my current situation likely amplified these impulses. I'm between jobs, have more unstructured time than usual, and I'm carrying both excitement and pressure about starting a new chapter in my career. Those emotions probably sought an outlet.

Looking back, I noticed that while I was fully engaged with work and personal goals, these urges were much easier to manage. It wasn't necessarily because they disappeared, but because I had meaningful challenges, daily structure, and people around me. My energy naturally flowed into something more fulfilling.

The biggest takeaway is that today wasn't a failure. It was another data point that helped me better understand what I'm actually looking for.

## Key Takeaways

- The anticipation is often more rewarding than the experience itself.
- Physical desire alone doesn't create lasting satisfaction.
- Genuine connection and trust matter far more than I had previously acknowledged.
- Unstructured time, uncertainty, and loneliness can amplify impulsive behavior.
- Having meaningful work, clear goals, and a structured routine naturally redirects my energy.
- Instead of judging myself, I should treat experiences like this as feedback that helps me better understand my own values.

> **Reminder for my future self:**  
> When I feel the urge to start searching again, remember that what I truly enjoyed last time was the anticipation—not the outcome. Pause and ask whether I'm actually seeking excitement, connection, purpose, or simply relief from boredom or uncertainty.
